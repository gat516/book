You are reading one chapter of a web novel for a reader's companion guide.

Write continuity notes for this chapter: what an editor would record so that later
chapters don't contradict it. That means the state of the story as this chapter leaves
it: who is alive, injured, missing or trapped; who holds which items and abilities;
who is allied with, loyal to or hostile towards whom; who knows or owes what; what was
promised or threatened; and rules of the world that were established. Note why a
state changed when the chapter says.

Before the chapter you are given what earlier chapters established:
- KNOWN: people, groups, places and named items, each with an ID like E3.
- NOTES: earlier notes that are still current, each with an ID like N1.4.

Use them to connect this chapter to the story so far:
- When a known one appears, refer to it by its ID, even if this chapter spells its
  name differently. Only you can judge whether it is the same one; a similar spelling
  is not enough and a different spelling does not rule it out.
- When this chapter changes, completes or contradicts an earlier note, list that
  note's ID under supersedes. Never repeat an earlier note that did not change.

Skip moment-to-moment action unless it changes the state of the story.

Think it through however you like first. Then write the heading `## Continuity` and,
below it, one note per line in this form:

category | status | names | supersedes | note

- category: one of character, relationship, faction, item, ability, world, open.
  Use "open" for anything left unresolved: threats, curses, invitations, grudges,
  unanswered questions.
- status: confirmed, expected, or uncertain. Use "confirmed" only when the chapter
  shows it. A fate that is only foreshadowed is "expected".
- names: semicolon-separated. A known one is its ID (E3). If this chapter spells a
  known one differently, write E3=New Spelling. Something new is +Name, spelled as the
  chapter spells it. People, groups, places and named items only; no numbers or
  descriptions.
- supersedes: earlier note IDs this note replaces, separated by semicolons, or - for none.
- note: one plain English sentence.

Examples:
item | confirmed | E1; +Crown of Ash | - | Alice now holds the Crown of Ash, taken from Bob.
open | expected | E2=Robert | N1.3 | Bob, now calling himself Robert, has sworn to take the crown back.

Nothing else goes below the heading: no bullets, bold, numbering or blank lines.
