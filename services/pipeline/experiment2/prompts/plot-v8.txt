You are reading one chapter of a web novel for a reader's companion guide.

Pick out the important plot points in this chapter: the developments a reader would
need to remember to follow the rest of the story. That means changes in who people
are, what they can do, who is allied with or against whom, what was gained or lost,
why characters made key decisions, promises, threats, unresolved loose ends, and
rules of the world that will matter later.

Skip moment-to-moment action and incidental details unless they change something that
matters later.

Before the chapter you are given what earlier chapters established:
- KNOWN: people, groups, places and named items, each with an ID like E3.
- NOTES: earlier plot points that still hold, each with an ID like N1.4.

Use them to connect this chapter to the story so far:
- When a known one appears, refer to it by its ID, even if this chapter spells its
  name differently. Only you can judge whether it is the same one; a similar spelling
  is not enough and a different spelling does not rule it out.
- When this chapter changes, completes or contradicts an earlier plot point, list that
  point's ID under supersedes. Never repeat an earlier plot point that did not change.

Think it through however you like first. Then write the heading `## Plot` and, below
it, one plot point per line in this form:

category | status | names | supersedes | plot point

- category: one of character, relationship, faction, item, ability, world, plot.
  Use another single word only if none of these fit.
- status: happened, ongoing, plan, threat, or implied. Use "implied" when the chapter
  strongly suggests something without showing it. Never mark a plan, threat or
  implication as happened.
- names: the people, groups, places and named items involved, separated by
  semicolons. A known one is its ID (E3). If this chapter spells a known one
  differently, write E3=New Spelling. Something new is +Name, spelled as the chapter
  spells it. No numbers or descriptions.
- supersedes: earlier plot point IDs this one replaces, separated by semicolons, or -
  for none.
- plot point: one plain English sentence.

Examples:
relationship | happened | E1; +Bob | - | Alice swore loyalty to Bob after he saved her life.
relationship | happened | E1; E2=Robert | N1.3 | Alice broke her oath after learning Bob, now calling himself Robert, had framed her.

Nothing else goes below the heading: no bullets, bold, numbering or blank lines.
